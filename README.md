It introduces "rariﬁcation" to evaluate rare word understanding, contributing improved representations for NLP tasks. • Word Embedding Techniques: Enhances BERT embeddings with AM, enabling early-layerinteractionforrarewords,improvingonshallowAMandstaticembeddings (e.g., Word2Vec, GloVe) by dynamically capturing context. • Performance Evaluation: Achieves up to 25% absolute improvement on rariﬁed MNLI datasets; MRR of 0.269 vs. BERT’s 0.112 on rare word probing tasks. • Strengths and Weaknesses: Strengths include robust handling of rare/mediumfrequency words and plug-and-play inference. Weaknesses include costly three-stage training and evaluation tied to baseline models. • Applications: Enhances text classiﬁcation, natural language inference (NLI), and ontology tasks, particularly in domains with rare terms (e.g., medical NLP). • Future Work: Extend beneﬁts to frequent words; integrate character-level CNNs for improved surface-form modeling.
2.2 Paper 2: Obtaining Better Static Word Embeddings Using Contextual Embedding Models (ACL 2021) • Summary: Tackles ineﬃciency of contextual models (e.g., BERT) for static embeddings in low-resource settings. Proposes X2STATIC distillation for high-quality static vectors, contributing eﬃcient alternatives for latency-critical NLP. • Word Embedding Techniques: Distills BERT/RoBERTa using CBOW-inspired averaging of sentence/paragraph contexts, improving on GloVe/Skip-gram by capturing broader semantics without order loss. • Performance Evaluation: Outperforms GloVe, FastText, and ASE on similarity tasks (e.g., SimLex-999) and downstream tasks (Macro-F1: 68.1 vs. 66.7 for ASE).

• Strengths and Weaknesses: Strengths include being 10 million times faster than contextual models and scalability. Weaknesses include limited hyperparameter tuning and potential for larger datasets. • Applications: Suitable for bias detection, sentiment analysis on edge devices, and hybrid systems with contextual models. • Future Work: Explore cross-lingual extensions, improved pooling methods, and sense-speciﬁc embeddings.
2.3 Paper 3: Improved Word Embeddings from Dictionary and Thesaurus Based on a Hybrid Method (COLING 2022) • Summary: Addresses corpus-heavy training by proposing HG2Vec, a graph-based embedding using dictionaries/thesauri, contributing corpus-free, eﬃcient learning balancing similarity and relatedness. • Word Embedding Techniques: Uses a heterogeneous graph with Word2Vec-like walksandcontext-focusedlossforsynonyms/antonyms,improvingonWord2Vec/Dict2Vec by modeling transitive relations. • PerformanceEvaluation: Achieves2973%SpearmanimprovementonMTurk-771/SimLex999; excels in relatedness tasks (20.8% better). • StrengthsandWeaknesses: Strengthsincluderesourceeﬃciencyandbalancedtask performance. Weaknesses include struggles with dual-meaning words. • Applications: Semantic similarity in recommender systems; NER with limited data. • Future Work: Incorporate derivative relations; leverage full dictionary deﬁnitions.
2.4 Paper 4: Using Context-to-Vector with Graph Retroﬁtting to Improve Word Embeddings (ACL 2022) • Summary: Improves static embeddings’ context capture with Context-to-Vec and retroﬁtting for Skip-gram, contributing lightweight enhancements for low-resource NLP. • Word Embedding Techniques: Combines Skip-gram with BERT-derived contextual attention and graph retroﬁtting via synonym lexicons/probability distributions, improving on Word2Vec by fusing syntax and semantics. • Performance Evaluation: Achieves +25.6% on similarity tasks vs. BERT+Skipgram; strong performance in chunking, NER, and POS tagging. • Strengths and Weaknesses: Strengths include modularity and interpretability. Weaknesses include minor analogy gains and a multi-stage process. • Applications: POS tagging, NER in mobile apps; analogy-based search systems.

• Future Work: Integrate priors during training for eﬃciency.

3 Reﬂection Report 3.1 Comparison of Embeddings In the sentiment analysis task on the IMDB dataset, FastText (dimension=300, 2 LSTM layers) achieved the best performance with 88% accuracy and 87% F1-score, compared to FastText (dimension=100, 1 layer: 85%/84%) and GloVe (dimension=100, 1 layer: 82%/81%). FastText’s subword n-grams eﬀectively handled rare words and misspellings commoninmoviereviews(e.g., "awesom"), aligningwiththeLOVEframework’sﬁndings on OOV robustness (Paper 5). GloVe struggled with OOV words due to its reliance on ﬁxed word vectors, lacking subword ﬂexibility, which supports the advancements highlighted in the reviewed papers.

3.2 Challenges Faced • Slow Training: Training on the full IMDB dataset was computationally intensive. This was mitigated by using a 25,000-sample subset and GPU acceleration. • Overﬁtting: Observed in deeper models; addressed by adding 0.5 dropout in the LSTM architecture. • Vanishing Gradients: Deeper RNNs showed instability; switching to GRU in experiments improved convergence.
3.3 Solutions and Improvements • Added bidirectional LSTM to capture context, improving accuracy by 3%. • Tuned hyperparameters, ﬁnding 2 layers optimal and learning rate of 0.0001 stable. • Introduced embedding dropout to enhance generalization.
